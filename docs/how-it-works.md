# How Studywise Courses works

## Courses

A course is a run of weeks, and each week has its own files (PDF slides, transcripts as .txt/.vtt/.srt, Word, PowerPoint, Markdown). Files are copied into the course's folder, so moving the originals doesn't break anything.

**Planning a week.** The week's files are read as text (PDFs with `[page N]` markers; scanned PDFs are sent as files instead) and sent with the outline prompt, along with the topics earlier weeks already have. A topic is usually 4–8 one-screen sections; a genuinely complex one can have up to 10, and a section can run to about two screens when an idea needs it. Over about 300k characters, the week is planned one file at a time and the results are merged by title. Topics get course-wide ids like `w2t3`. Planning a week again replaces its topics. Adding files never starts planning by itself: making a course, adding a week, **Add files** and dropping files on a week all add the files and then ask **Plan now?** (or **Not now**, to add more files first). The plan dialog has a box for your own instructions ("skip the guest lecture"), which are sent worded so they take priority over the planning prompt's rules.

**Note for the AI.** Each course can have a note (course page, or ⋯ → Add a note for the AI), such as "this course is coding heavy and in C, so teach the theory and the coding together". It's added to every prompt for the course: planning, writing topics, marking answers, trimming cards, and Explain and Learn on its week decks. It can also be filled in when making the course.

**Levels of detail.** Topics can be written at four levels: **Standard** (the usual notes), **Quick revision** (shorter sections, mostly bullets and tables, still covering nearly every examinable point), **In-depth** (a fuller write-up that goes slowly, with more worked examples and "Check yourself" lines, for topics you struggle with) and **Explain it like I'm 12** (`simple`: everyday words, short sentences and a simple everyday example for every idea, one small step per section ending "In one line:", with "Quick check:" lines; still covers every examinable point, with the exact wording of formal definitions after the simple version). Each level's extra rules are in `levelInstructions` in `src/main/services/course.ts`. The Write dialog lets you tick one or more; each level is a separate AI call and every level written is kept. A topic written at more than one level shows a Standard / Quick revision / In-depth switch on the week page; your progress is kept separately for each level, and the topic's note follows the level shown. ⋯ → Write at another level…, or the ↻ button inside a topic, adds a level (or rewrites one). The recall cards stay with the topic: once it's written at one level, other levels reuse the same cards, so their review history isn't lost and you don't get near-duplicates. **Quick review** on the week page (next to Write, once a topic is written) writes the quick revision level for every written topic that doesn't have one yet, and **Add a quick review** under a topic does it for that topic. A quick review is saved alongside the notes you already have, which stay shown (`write` with `keepShown`), and **Show the week's notes as** switches every topic of the week between the levels it has. Every level is in the `.studyweek` file (`topics.versions`), so sharing or publishing the week sends the notes and the quick review together, and the marketplace shows each topic's levels.

**Writing a topic.** Only the topic's own week's files are sent, and PDFs are cut down to the pages in the outline's `sources` (a short PDF with no page range goes whole). Figures come back as a page in that excerpt and are mapped to the original page, then rendered to a PNG with pdf.js and cached. Questions that can't be asked as written are dropped. Recall cards go into that week's deck, tagged with the topic, replacing that topic's old cards. A topic gets only the few cards worth keeping for the exam (usually 4–8: key definitions, lists, steps, contrasts, formulas and anything stressed; no examples, illustrations, stories or non-examinable parts). A card whose answer is a list says how many points it wants, "(2)" for two bullets: the app adds or corrects that itself, and fixes older cards (and the same cards inside their topics) at startup.

**Flashcards by week.** Making a course makes a folder for it in Decks, and every week gets its own deck there ("Physics 101 · Week 1"), so the cards are grouped the way the course is. In Decks (and in the Notes sidebar), "All" groups items under their folders; click a folder to collapse it, and the app remembers which are collapsed. Notes folders can hold folders of their own (hover a folder in the sidebar and press the folder-plus button), and picking a folder shows everything inside it. Each written topic's notes go in the course's notes folder, in a folder for its week ("Physics 101 › Week 3"); notes from before that are moved there on startup, unless you've moved them somewhere else. Renaming the course or a week renames the folder and decks; deleting a week deletes its deck. Recall cards on the course page reviews what's due across all its weeks, and each week's page opens its own deck. Course → ⋯ → Models & instructions applies to every week deck at once, and new weeks pick it up. Courses made before this had one deck for everything: on startup their cards move into week decks (with their review history), and any cards you added yourself stay in a "· Other cards" deck in the same folder.

**Weeks.** The course page lists its weeks, one row each, with how many topics, files and flashcards it has and how far you've got. Weeks are in week-number order, taken from their labels ("Week 3", "W3", "Lecture 3"), whatever order they were added in, so a week added later (one downloaded from the marketplace, say) sits in its place; a week with no number ("Reading week") stays after the week it was added after. **Week 1 first** / **Latest week first** above the list picks the direction for every course, remembered on the device. Only the order shown changes: a week's position (which topic ids like `w3t1` and "earlier weeks" when planning use) stays as it was. Click a week to open it: its topics come first, each with its sections listed underneath (ticked once you're past them; click one to open the topic at that section), then its files (click to open the course's copy, or show it in its folder), then its flashcards. Planning, writing, adding files, renaming and deleting the week all happen there, and the arrows at the bottom go to the week before or after.

**Adding new notes to a week.** **Add new notes** on a planned week's page (and in its ⋯ menu) takes pasted notes (saved in the week as `Notes added <date>.md`) and/or files, and starts a job (`addNotesToWeek`). One call (`course_update`) compares the new material with the week's topics and returns only what's new: new sections for existing topics, and new topics for separate ideas. A topic that isn't written yet just gets the new sections in its plan. A written topic gets only the new sections (`course_extend`, which uses the same writing rules as a topic and is sent the existing sections so the new ones fit after them), at every level it's written at, appended after its own sections with `added` set to when they were added. Nothing already there is rewritten, so what you've studied stays as it was. New recall cards are made once and shared by every level; a few topic-test questions for the new material are added to the check. A topic you'd finished opens again at the first new section (your scores are kept), so Continue goes straight to what's new. Until you've reviewed them, new sections show **New** in the week's section list, the topic shows "N new sections" and the week "N new sections to review" (`unreviewedSections`); new topics show **New topic**; and the section itself says "New · added <date>". Sharing or publishing the week leaves the `added` marks out: they're about your own studying, like progress.

**Learning.** Each section shows its notes, then that section's flashcards (the key terms), then its questions, so you know the definitions before you're tested. For each card you think of the answer, then turn it over (click it or press Space, and as often as you like, back and forth), and rate yourself: Didn't know (1, red), Sort of (2, yellow) or Knew it (3, green). Your first rating of each card goes into spaced repetition. A card is done once you've pressed Knew it twice: red brings it back after three more cards, yellow sends it to the back of the pile, and green sends it to the back too, with the second green finishing it. Greens always count, whatever colours came before (the dots by the card show how many it has). After Didn't know or Sort of you choose how to go over it: **Next**, **Re-read the notes** (the section's notes again, no AI) or **Explain it with AI** (a page that goes through it properly: plain meaning, the exact definition, why it matters, a worked example, the usual mistake and a way to remember it; one Explain call, only when you ask). **Back to the card** returns from either page. An "In your own words" question (an essay question marked by Claude) says what it's out of: a mark for each key point in its mark scheme (`explainMarks`). Claude awards whole marks, the result shows them (for example 2 / 3 marks), and **I was right** gives full marks. **Hint** (or H) is optional and gives hints one at a time: the question's nudge first, then for an essay question each point the mark scheme gives a mark for (`questionHints`). The ← button at the top (or B) goes back a step anywhere in a topic, for example from the questions to the notes; answers you already gave stay as they were. Topics written before this have no per-section cards and go straight to the questions; rewrite one (↻) to get them. Notes can include diagrams Claude draws itself as ```svg blocks; they're shown as images, so nothing inside them can run. **I don't get this** under a section's notes opens Explain with just that section: nothing runs until you type a question or pick Simpler, Example and so on, and only the section's notes are sent (no PDFs), so it's cheap. The ↻ button at the top rewrites the whole topic, with an optional note on what to change; that costs as much as writing it the first time, replaces its recall cards and resets its progress. The topic test is separate: the **Test** button on the week page (or **Take the test** when you finish a topic) asks the topic's 5–8 harder questions from memory, with no notes, and the week page shows your last score. Only your first try at each question counts. Multiple choice, true/false, fill in the blank (with alternatives), spot the wrong word, put in order, match and sort are marked in code; "in your own words" answers are marked by Claude out of 10. "I was right" overrides any mark, and **Undo** (or U) puts the original mark back. Multiple-choice answers are in a new random order each time you do a topic or its test again, and a question that comes back to try again always has its right answer in a different place (Learn does the same, and cached Learn questions are reshuffled too). Your place is saved at every step (the notes, a flashcard or a particular question), so **Save & exit** (or Esc) and **Continue** on the week page carry on exactly where you were. While the app is open the whole session is kept too (your answers, questions waiting to come back, where you are in a pile of key-term cards), so leaving a topic for another tab and coming back changes nothing; the **Courses** tab itself takes you back to the topic, week or course you were last on (click it again for the list). Picking a section on the week page starts that section fresh, and finishing or resetting a topic lets its session go. After a section there's a 1 in 4 chance of a recall card from another topic you've started, due cards first; your answer goes into spaced repetition. A question you get wrong (or only partly right) comes back three steps later, marked "Try again", and keeps coming back until you get it right; only your first try counts towards the topic's score.

**Cards wait until you study them.** Writing a topic doesn't add its cards to your reviews. A card joins spaced review when you reach its section's flashcards in the topic (or rate it, or finish the topic); until then it isn't due anywhere. Cards you've reviewed never go back to waiting; resetting a topic's progress puts its never-reviewed cards back. Cards from older topics without per-section cards join when you start the topic.

**Exam dates.** Course → ⋯ → **Set exam date…**. More than 6 weeks before the exam, reviews keep going as usual but spaced 1.5× further apart, so fewer come up each day. For the last 6 weeks revision ramps them up: no card goes longer than a shrinking maximum gap without a review (10 days six weeks out, 3 days two weeks out, every day for the last few), cards scheduled further out are pulled back in, and no review lands later than 2 days before the exam, so every card gets a last look. After the exam the course rests. The course page shows where it is ("Exam 5 Feb · in 75 days · steady recall, revision from 25 Dec").

**Trimming recall cards.** A week's ⋯ menu has **Trim recall cards…** (each topic's menu has the same for just that topic, and the course's menu has it for the whole course at once, which also catches the same card in two different weeks). Claude gets only the cards' text, no slides, so it's cheap, and suggests every card that isn't pure course content (examples, companies or people used as illustrations, stories, opinions, anything about the course or exam, trivia, duplicates), each with a reason. Nothing goes until you've looked: untick any you want to keep, then remove the rest, which also takes them out of their topic's flashcards and your spaced review.

**Turning recall off for a week.** A week's ⋯ menu has **Turn off recall for this week**. Its cards stay, with their review history, but stop coming up: no surprise recall cards from it while you learn other topics, nothing from it in the course's Recall cards review, and none of its cards in due counts (Home, Decks, the Dock badge). You can still open its deck and review it by hand. The week shows **Recall off** on the course and week pages; turn it back on from the same menu (or that label).

**Adding to a week.** Drop files anywhere on a week's page (or use **Add files**): PDF, Word, PowerPoint, text, Markdown, subtitles (.vtt, .srt), web pages (.html), RTF, CSV, or pictures of slides or the whiteboard (PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF), which Claude looks at like a scanned PDF (with Ollama, pictures need a model with vision). Old .doc/.ppt and Keynote files need saving as PDF first. Dropping a shared `.studyweek` there merges it into that week: its topics come after the week's own, numbered on (`w2t4`, `w2t5`, …), with their cards and files.

**Sharing a week.** A week's ⋯ menu has **Share this week…**, which saves a `.studyweek` file: the week's topics (notes, flashcards, questions and test), its recall cards and its source files (slides and transcripts, which topics need for their figures and for writing a topic again). Your progress, reviews and course settings stay behind. Whoever gets it adds it with **Add a shared week** on the Courses page (or the course's ⋯ menu, or by dropping or opening the file), into one of their courses or a new one named like yours. It goes in as that course's next week, with new ids, topic numbers to match its position (`w3t1` becomes `w5t1` as week 5), and a number on any week label or file name they already have. Nothing is generated, so it costs no tokens; topics that weren't written yet can be written there as normal. Slide figures aren't in the file: they're rendered again from the PDF when shown.

**The marketplace.** Settings → Marketplace connects to a marketplace site ([studywise-server](https://github.com/SnakeLuck360GT/studywise-server), which anyone can host): paste its address and an API key made on its account page (the key is encrypted like the Ollama key). The Marketplace page (a button on Courses) lists courses people have published, with who made them and which models wrote them. A course there belongs to nobody: everyone's weeks for the same course go in it, and each week belongs to whoever published it, so publishing your Week 1 again replaces only yours; open one to see its weeks (when several people have published the same week, it shows once with how many people generated it; click it to open every version, most downloaded first, each with its own **Add to my courses**. Weeks count as the same by the number in their label, so one person's "Week 3" and another's "W3: Testing" go together even if they sit at different places in their courses; see `weeksByWeek` in `src/shared/market.ts`), expand a week to see its topics and sections, and **Add to my courses** downloads it and adds it like a shared week. Your own published weeks have the button too, which is how you get them onto another device (or one using a different API key); a week of yours added that way is linked to what you published, so publishing it from there updates the same marketplace week and it can be published automatically. The app remembers which marketplace weeks it has (added, or published from this device; `market.added` and `market.published`): those show **In <course>** (or **Updated since you added it** when it's been published again since), and adding one again first says where you already have it and asks before making a second copy. A whole course downloads at once: **Download missing weeks** / **Download all weeks** on its page (or the download button on its card) asks which of your courses it goes into (the same course, matched by module code or name, is picked for you; or a new one), says what's coming, and adds the weeks one after another as a job. Weeks are matched by week number ("Week 3", "W3"), or by label when there's no number. Where several people published the same week only one is added: the one you already added to that course, else yours, else the most written (then most downloaded, newest). A week you already added to that course is always left out; **missing** also leaves out any week the course has with topics in it, yours or not. A blank week of yours with the same number is filled instead of adding another. The app remembers which of your courses each marketplace course's weeks went into (`market.courseLinks`, set whenever you add a week of it, or publish a week of yours to it; for courses from before that, the course holding a week of it you already have). After that, adding one of its weeks or downloading it doesn't ask which course: the confirm says where it's going, with **Somewhere else…** to pick another (which then becomes the remembered one). A deleted course is forgotten. **Published by you** has the same two buttons for everything you've published, each course going into the course you put it in before, else your course of the same name, else a new one, which is how you set up a new computer. A week's ⋯ menu has **Publish to marketplace…**, which uploads the same `.studyweek` (topics, cards, slides and transcripts, no progress); publishing it again replaces it. Settings → Marketplace → **Publish my weeks automatically** (off until you turn it on) publishes a week 20 seconds after one of its topics finishes writing, once none of its topics are still being written, and again whenever you write more. A week that a shared or marketplace week went into holds someone else's work, so it's never published automatically. Courses and weeks are public unless you make them private (`courses.private`, `weeks.private`): the **Visibility** setting in New course and Add a week, or **Make … private** in their ⋯ menus. A new week starts with its course's setting. A week is private if it or its course is, and a private week is never published, whether by hand, in bulk or automatically. Making a published week (or its course) private takes it down from the marketplace, and making it public again queues it for automatic publishing if that's on. Visibility syncs to the phone but isn't put in a `.studyweek` file. Courses on the marketplace match under different names: by module code when both names have one ("Software Engineering 1 (COMP23311)" and "Software Engineering 1" are the same course, COMP23311 and CS1001 never are), otherwise by title without the code, brackets, punctuation or case, and failing that a week made from the same slides as a published week joins that week's course. Each planned week records the model and effort that planned it (`weeks.planned_with`), and each topic the model and effort that wrote each of its levels (`topics.written_with`), so the marketplace can show them. **Publish all my courses** (and a course's **Publish course to marketplace…**) only uploads weeks that are new to the marketplace, were taken down there, or have changed since you published them; the rest are skipped and the dialog says how many. A week counts as changed when `weekContentHash` (in `src/main/services/weekShare.ts`) differs from the hash saved when it was published (`market.publishedHash`). The hash covers what's sent (topics, every level of detail, cards, the week and course details, and each file's bytes) but not studying (progress, cards waking up, timestamps) or settings that aren't shared (recall on or off, private). Weeks published before hashes were kept are compared once with the site's summary of them (label, topic, written and card counts, file names and sizes) and remembered from then on. Publishing automatically skips a week that hasn't changed too. Publishing one week from its ⋯ menu always uploads it.

When you add a week, and before a week with no topics is planned, the app asks the marketplace whether someone has already made it. Weeks that are still blank (no topics) keep asking: the course page marks them **Already made** when someone has published them, the week page shows a banner with **Use this week…**, and the week's ⋯ menu has **Find on marketplace…** to look again. Answers are kept for five minutes, and a week you answered "I'll make it myself" for stops showing the banner until the app restarts. It sends the course name, the week's label and a fingerprint of its files: a MinHash of their text (128 numbers, from five-word shingles, see `src/shared/fingerprint.ts`) and a sha256 of each file. The files themselves aren't sent. A published week matches if it was made from mostly the same material, or if it's the same week number ("Week 9") of what looks like the same course: the same module code, or titles that mostly share their words, so "Databases" finds "Database Systems (COMP23111)" but "Software Engineering 1" never finds "Software Engineering 2". If someone else has published one (weeks published from this device don't count, since you have them already; your own from another device do), a dialog shows it (how much is the same, how many topics are written, which model) and offers **Use this week**, which merges it into your week, or **Plan it myself anyway**. Merging a week into one that already has the same file (same bytes) uses your copy rather than adding a numbered duplicate. With no marketplace connected, or the site down, planning goes ahead as usual.

**Models.** The three course tasks are in Settings → Models like every other task (any model name, any effort). Course → ⋯ → Models & instructions overrides them for one course and adds extra instructions to its prompts.

`npm run ai:course-check` plans a week and writes a topic from the test PDF with real Claude calls (Sonnet by default, `-- --model=opus` to change). `node scripts/course-shots.mjs [dir]` walks through a mock course and screenshots each step.


## Talking to Claude

Every AI feature goes through one function, `runAI()` in `src/main/ai/`. It looks up the task's prompt and model, fills in the template, runs Claude Code, and checks what comes back.

Each prompt's fixed instructions, and the JSON format for JSON tasks, go in the system prompt, while the message only has what changes (the module, the files, the topic). Claude caches a repeated system prompt, so writing several topics, or the parts of a long PDF, reads those few thousand tokens from cache instead of paying for them again. Each call is `claude -p` in a new empty temp folder: prompt on stdin, `stream-json` output, no tools, limited turns, and no user settings or MCP servers. Structured tasks use `--json-schema` and the result is checked again with zod. If it doesn't fit, the reply and the problem are sent back on their own (no files or sources) to be corrected; only if that fails is the whole request run again. PDFs are the only exception to "no tools": the file is copied into the temp folder and only the read-only `Read` tool is allowed, so Claude sees the real pages, images included.

`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` and similar variables are removed from the environment, so calls always use your Claude login and never an API account. Apps opened from Finder don't get your shell's `PATH`, so on startup Studywise asks your login shell for it, then also checks `~/.claude/local`, `~/.local/bin`, Homebrew, `/usr/local/bin` and npm's global bin. Settings → Claude Code shows what it found and lets you pick the binary yourself.

Calls run three at a time, time out after 120 seconds (15 minutes for generation), and retry once on network or overload errors. Each one is logged with its task, prompt version, model, time and tokens under Settings → AI usage. The plan-usage numbers Claude Code reports (5-hour and weekly) are shown in the top bar.

Long jobs (generating notes or cards) run in the main process. Closing the window doesn't stop them, you get a notification when one finishes, and the Jobs page can cancel them.

## Updating

A copy run from its git checkout (`npm run dev` or `npm start`) keeps itself up to date. Shortly after launch and every 30 minutes it runs `git fetch` and compares the branch with GitHub. When there are new commits, a banner under the top bar says how many and lists them. **Update & restart** runs `git pull --ff-only`, then `npm install` if `package.json` or `package-lock.json` changed, then quits and starts the app again with the same npm script. The new run is started from a detached helper, so it carries on after the old terminal session ends; its output goes to `logs/restart.log` in the data folder.

It won't start while a job is running. Uncommitted edits are kept unless they clash with the update, in which case nothing changes and the banner says so. If the branch has its own commits that aren't on GitHub, the banner tells you to merge by hand. **Later** hides the banner until more changes arrive. Installed builds don't show it.

An installed copy updates from the GitHub releases instead (electron-updater, configured by `publish` in `electron-builder.config.cjs`). Shortly after launch, every 30 minutes, and when you come back to the window more than 10 minutes after the last check, it reads `latest.yml` (Windows) or `latest-mac.yml` (Mac) from the newest release; Settings → About has **Check for updates** to check now. On Windows a newer version downloads in the background; the banner then offers **Restart to update**, and otherwise it installs the next time you quit. If the download fails the banner says so and **Try again** downloads it again (the next check also retries). **Later** hides the banner until the update is ready or a newer version comes out. electron-updater's log goes to `logs/main.log` (lines starting `[update]`). Mac builds aren't signed with a Developer ID, and macOS won't let an unsigned app replace itself, so there the banner says a new version is out and **Download** opens its release. With a Developer ID certificate (see the top of `electron-builder.config.cjs`) the Mac could install updates itself too. `scripts/release.sh` uploads the `latest*.yml` files and blockmaps with the installers; a release without them isn't seen by installed copies. It creates the release as a draft, uploads the installers and blockmaps, then the `latest*.yml` files, and publishes it only at the end, so an installed copy never sees a release that's missing its files.

## Using Ollama instead

Settings → AI provider switches every AI feature between Claude Code and Ollama. Ollama means Ollama Cloud (`https://ollama.com`, with an API key) or your own server (`http://localhost:11434`, which can also run `-cloud` models when you're signed in). The key is encrypted with the OS keychain (DPAPI on Windows), stored in the database as `secret.ollamaKey`, and never sent to the page. `OLLAMA_API_KEY` works too.

Calls go to `/api/chat`, streamed. Files can't be handed over as tools, so they're read in the app: text goes in `<file>` blocks (PDFs with `[page N]` markers), and PDFs with no text layer are sent as page images (24 at most), which needs a model with the `vision` capability. Cloud models don't support structured output, so JSON tasks rely on the schema in the prompt plus the usual zod check and one retry; local models also get `format` set to the schema and a bigger `num_ctx` (32k by default, changeable). Auto-continue keeps the conversation in memory. Thinking/effort maps to Ollama's `think` option: off, or low/medium/high (gpt-oss takes the level, other models just turn thinking on, and a model without thinking is retried without it). Inline `<think>` blocks are hidden.

Settings → Models lists the server's models (`/api/tags`) with their capabilities. Star a model to save it, and saved models come first in every picker; you can also save a model name that isn't listed. Pick a default model, then optionally a model and thinking level per task. Decks have their own Ollama overrides (stored as `ollama:<task>` in the deck's model overrides), and Claude settings are kept separately, so switching back loses nothing. A custom action whose model is a Claude name uses the task's Ollama model.

### On Windows

GUI apps get the full user PATH, so Studywise just looks there and in `%APPDATA%\npm` and `~\.local\bin`. npm installs `claude` as a `claude.cmd` shim, which can't be started without a shell, so Studywise runs the package's `cli.js` with `node.exe` directly. The native installer's `claude.exe` runs as is. The window uses Windows' own caption buttons, shortcuts show as Ctrl/Alt/Shift, and closing the window quits the app once no job is running. Data lives in `%APPDATA%\Studywise`.

## Plan usage per job

Each job (writing a topic, planning a week, generating notes or cards) shows how much of your Claude plan's 5-hour limit it used. Claude Code reports the 5-hour usage with every call, in whole percents and as it stood before that call, so the job takes the first reading during the job as "before", then makes one tiny Haiku call when it finishes to get a fresh "after". The difference shows in a toast, on the Jobs page and on the topic's row. It's marked as shared when other jobs ran at the same time, and it can't tell apart other use of your plan in the same minutes (Claude Code in a terminal, claude.ai). If the 5-hour window resets during a job, nothing is shown. Not measured with Ollama.

## Notes and flashcards

**Revision order.** Notes are listed in each folder in the order you'd revise them, not newest first (`notes.position`, `src/main/services/noteOrder.ts`). A course topic's notes go by week, then the topic's place in the week. Other notes go by the week they're for (the note's Week, or "Week 3" / "W3" in the title), then the lecture number in the title ("Lecture 5", "L5"), then when they were made; a note with no week goes after the numbered ones. New notes, from writing a topic or generating notes, are put in place as they're made, and a note moved to another folder takes its place there. Notes made before this have no position and are listed after the ordered ones, newest first, until you use **Revision order** above the notes list, which orders the selected folder (and the folders inside it), or every folder when All notes is selected.

PDFs are split into 20-page chunks, the most Claude Code's Read tool takes in one go. Notes, flashcards and course topics leave out anything the material itself marks as not examinable ("non-examinable", "not assessed", "optional", "for interest only"), and keep anything that's unclear. For notes, each chunk is told what's already been written and the pieces are joined into one note that streams into the editor. Picking several sources switches to the "Linking texts" preset, which sends them in one call so they can be compared.

Cards come back in the StudySmarter text format (below). If a reply gets cut off, the half-finished card is dropped and Claude is told to "continue" in the same session, up to 10 times, with duplicates removed. Every card is checked: the `(n)` in a question has to match the number of bullets, fill-in-the-blank cards need one answer per blank and no `(n)`, and empty sides, duplicates and missing separators are flagged. Anything flagged gets a **Fix with AI** button in the preview.

## Learn mode

Each card becomes one of eight question types: Read & understand, Multiple choice, Free answer, True or false, Match pairs, Categorize, Fill in the blank and Choice blank. Blanks come from `___` in cloze cards or **bold** words in an answer. Questions are made eight cards per call when the session starts, the next batch is fetched ahead of time, and each question is cached per card and type until the card or prompt changes.

Multiple-choice options are shuffled in code (here and in course topics), so the right answer's position gives nothing away, and the prompts ask for options of the same length and level of detail, so the right one isn't simply the longest or most careful-sounding. Multiple choice, true/false, match pairs, categorize and choice blank are marked in code. Free answers are scored out of 10 by Claude against the card, with credit per required point (8+ correct, 4–7 partly right, 3 or less wrong; you can change these). Fill in the blank checks for an exact match first and only asks Claude about the blanks that don't match. Every result can be overridden with "I was right". A question you get wrong comes back three questions later, marked "Try again", and keeps coming back until it's right (Skip moves on without it). The retry is marked as normal but doesn't change the schedule or the session's score, since your first answer already did.

After an answer, **Get feedback** (or `w`) writes why yours was wrong, the key takeaways and a way to remember it. Nothing is generated until you press it. Hints come from the card first, then Claude, two at most, and never give away the answer.

Explain only runs when you ask. Open it on a card, a feedback panel or selected note text, then choose Explain, Simpler, Go deeper, Example, Diagram or Why was I wrong, or type a question. The answer can be saved to the card, saved to your notes (the whole conversation as a note, in a folder you pick, "Saved explanations" by default) or turned into new cards. **Back** (or Esc) closes it and returns to what you were doing.

## Offline mode

**Offline** at the top of the window (or Settings → General → Offline mode, or View → Offline Mode) turns off every AI call: `runAI` refuses while it's on (`offline.enabled`, `src/main/offline.ts`), so nothing reaches Claude or Ollama, however it was started. It's a setting on each device, so it doesn't sync: a phone can be offline while the computer isn't. On the phone, the top bar shows **Offline** while it's on.

Studying only asks what can be made and marked without AI. In Learn, free-answer and sorting questions are left out of the session. Each question is the one the AI made for that card earlier (from the question cache, any version of the prompt) when its type is marked in code; otherwise multiple choice takes its wrong options from other cards' answers in the deck (picked from the ones closest in length, with images and bold taken out) or the card's own options, choice blanks take theirs from other cards' blanks, and fill in the blank and match pairs are made as usual. A card nothing fits is shown to read. A blank only counts when it matches exactly (no AI for near misses), with "I was right" for answers that mean the same. Hints come from the card only; Regenerate, Explain, Get feedback and custom actions are hidden. Turning offline mode on or off starts the Learn session again. In a course topic, written-answer questions (`explain`) are left out of the steps and the test (the test says how many), other questions keep their numbers so your place still resumes, and Rewrite, Explain and "I don't get this" are hidden. A test that's all written answers says to turn offline mode off. On the phone, Learn runs on the phone's own copy while offline, so it works with no computer; requests that would have the computer call the AI stop on the phone.

## Models

Settings → Models sets a model and effort level for each task, with a Test button. Defaults: opus for notes and writing course topics; sonnet for planning course topics (it only reads text); haiku for marking blanks, hints and the quick note edits; sonnet for everything else. A deck can override any of these, and its "extra instructions" are added to every prompt for that deck.

## Prompts and custom actions

Every task's prompts live in Settings → Prompts. Built-in prompts you haven't edited follow the app's updates; once you edit one it's never overwritten, and Reset makes it follow updates again. You can edit them, see exactly what Claude will receive, test-run them, duplicate them, set a default, view the history with a diff, and reset built-ins. For JSON tasks the output format is added by the app, so a prompt edit can't break parsing.

Custom actions are your own AI buttons: a name, a prompt, a model, whether the result is Markdown or new cards, and where the button shows up (cards, note selection, Learn questions, after answering). Presets and actions export and import together as JSON.

## Spaced repetition

Two schedulers, switchable in Settings → General.

**Difficulty-based (default)**, modelled on real scheduling data: cards start at difficulty 0.60 with three 1-day steps, then intervals grow quickly (roughly 3, 8, 20, 115, 280 days and on) and shrink for harder cards. A card comes back after `interval × (0.694 − 0.187 × difficulty)` days. Success lowers difficulty, and forgetting a card sends it back to relearning.

**SM-2**: ease starts at 2.5, intervals go 1, 6, then interval × ease.

Flashcard mode uses Again / Hard / Good / Easy, Learn results feed the same schedule, and the Dock badge shows how many cards are due today. A card you mark Again comes back three cards later in the same session, and again each time, until you know it.

## Import and export

- **StudySmarter text**: `question/#*#/answer`, with cards separated by a line that's just `#`. A trailing `(n)` sets the required points, and `___` runs make a cloze card whose answers are the `- ` bullets. Round-trips exactly.
- **StudySmarter Excel**: Question, Answer A–F each followed by its "answer is correct" flag (TRUE/FALSE, `=TRUE()`, true/false or 1/0), then Tags, Hints and Explanation. A normal card exports as Answer A marked TRUE.
- Deck JSON and CSV.
- Notes from Notion or Obsidian: a single .md file, or a .zip of a Notion export or an Obsidian vault. Properties come from frontmatter (Obsidian) or the lines under the title (Notion) and fill Module, Type and Week; other frontmatter properties stay with the note. Notion's callouts (`<aside>`) and toggles (`<details>`) become callouts, links to other Notion pages become `[[links]]`, and images (Notion's relative paths, Obsidian's `![[embeds]]`, found next to the note or anywhere in the zip) are copied into the app. Notes show Obsidian's syntax: `> [!type]` callouts (foldable with + or -), `==highlights==`, `[[links]]` that open the note with that title, hidden `%%comments%%`, and frontmatter as a small properties table. **Export for Obsidian** writes frontmatter properties and native syntax, with images and drawn diagrams as files in `attachments/` next to the note. **Export for Notion** writes the title as a heading, properties as lines, callouts as quotes with an icon, highlights as bold and `[[links]]` as plain text; with images it's a .zip for Notion's Markdown import.
- **Anki** `.apkg` and `.colpkg`, both the old format (`collection.anki2`/`.anki21`, note types as JSON) and the current one (`collection.anki21b`, zstd-compressed, note types and media list in protobuf). Every Anki card becomes a card here: the card template is rendered (fields, `{{#…}}` sections, `text:`/`type:`/`hint:` filters, `{{FrontSide}}`), the answer side is cut at `<hr id=answer>`, and the HTML becomes Markdown, including bold, lists, sub/superscript, MathJax `\(…\)` and `[$]…[/$]` as KaTeX, and images copied into attachments. Sounds are dropped. Cloze notes become fill-in-the-blank cards, one per cloze number, with hints kept and "Back Extra" as the explanation. Image occlusion cards are skipped. Current Anki marks text columns with its own `unicase` collation, which SQLite outside Anki doesn't have; the importer swaps it for `NOCASE` in its temporary copy so the note type tables can be read. Import as new decks makes one deck per Anki deck (subdecks as "Parent › Child", in a folder named after the file when there are several), and importing into a deck puts everything there. Review progress comes too (interval, ease, reps, lapses, due date, last review, and FSRS difficulty when there is one) unless you turn it off in Settings → General. Drop an `.apkg` on the window or open it with Studywise to import it straight away.

PDF, Word and PowerPoint files can be dropped on the window or the Dock icon, or opened with **Open With → Studywise**.

## Your data

Everything is in `~/Library/Application Support/Studywise/` (on Windows, `%APPDATA%\Studywise`): the SQLite database, pasted images, a cache and logs. Settings → General can open the folder, back the whole library up to a zip, and restore it (the old database is kept alongside).

**Updating.** Run the new installer over the old one: it replaces the app wherever it was installed and never touches the data folder (on Windows that's `%APPDATA%\Studywise Courses`; uninstalling leaves it too). The first time a new version starts, it copies the database into `backups/before-<version>-…/` before migrating it, keeping the last five. To go back, quit the app and copy that `studywise.db` over the one in the data folder. A copy run from git counts a new commit (an update from the banner, or a pull) as a new version too, so it's backed up the same way.

The installer runs the old version's uninstaller first. Up to 1.8.7 the desktop build shipped the phone app's Capacitor packages, Android build output included, in `resources/app.asar.unpacked`, with paths past Windows' 260-character limit, which the uninstaller can't move or delete ("Failed to uninstall old application files…: 2"). `build/installer.nsh` deletes that folder with a long-path-safe `rd \?\…` before the old uninstaller runs, and `electron-builder.config.cjs` leaves `node_modules/@capacitor` out of the desktop build. Keep anything with deep paths out of `app.asar.unpacked`.

**Moving to another computer.** With both computers on the same network, Settings → Devices → Link to another computer does it without a file (see Linking two computers, below). Otherwise, Settings → General → Move to another computer exports everything to one `.studywise` file that works on Mac and Windows: decks, cards, notes, folders, courses with their copied files and figures, pasted images, prompts and custom actions. Progress (review history, due dates, where you are in each topic) only goes in if you tick "Include my progress". Importing (the same card, or dropping the file on the window) adds to the library that's there and never replaces anything; everything gets new ids, so importing twice gives two copies rather than a clash. Settings and API keys stay behind. Inside, it's a zip of `library.json`, `attachments/` and `courses/<id>/`, with course file paths stored relative to the course and file names made safe for both systems.

## Phone app and sync

The Android app (`src/mobile`, wrapped by Capacitor in `android/`) runs the same React app as the desktop, with a phone layout: a slim top bar, a tab bar at the bottom, study screens that take the whole screen, and keyboard hints, file pickers and desktop-only settings left out. Underneath, the desktop's main-process code (repo, course progress, learning) runs in the page on sql.js, SQLite compiled to WebAssembly; `vite.mobile.config.ts` swaps the database module and the Node, Electron, AI and file modules for phone versions. The database is saved to IndexedDB a moment after each write and when the app goes to the background.

`window.studywise` on the phone has the same channels as the desktop. Studying, reviews, decks, cards, notes and course progress run on the phone's copy. Anything that needs the computer (AI calls, writing topics, rendering slide figures, file imports) is sent to it: the phone syncs first, the computer runs the request through the same handlers as its own window, then the phone syncs again. Streamed text and job progress come back through a long poll. Figures and pasted images are fetched once and kept on the phone, so topics show them offline; figures for written topics are fetched after each sync.

**Syncing.** Thirteen tables sync (folders, decks, cards, notes and their deck links, review state and history, learn sessions and answers, courses, weeks, course files and topics). Settings, prompts, AI logs and caches stay on each device. Triggers record every write to a synced table in `sync_log` with a sequence number, a timestamp and the device it came from, so no other code knows about syncing. The phone sends the rows it changed since the computer last saw its log, then applies the computer's rows changed since it last looked (leaving out the phone's own changes). When both changed the same row, the later change wins. Deletes and cascades travel the same way; a row whose parent was deleted on the other side is removed (or its link cleared, for `ON DELETE SET NULL`). The first sync sends the whole library. The phone syncs when it opens, when it comes back to the front, every minute, a few seconds after a change, and before and after anything it asks the computer to do.

**iPhone.** The same phone app also builds for iOS (`ios/`, Swift Package Manager, no CocoaPods), with the Studywise icon made full-bleed and opaque for iOS (`npm run icons:ios`). Sync is plain HTTP to the computer on the local network: `Info.plist` allows that for local network addresses only (`NSAllowsLocalNetworking`; everything else still needs HTTPS), and the first sync makes iOS ask to use the local network. It syncs whenever it can reach the computer: when the app opens or comes back to the front, every minute while it's open, and a few seconds after a change. To put it on your iPhone:

1. Install Xcode from the Mac App Store, open it once (accept the licence, let it install its components), then point the command-line tools at it: `sudo xcode-select -s /Applications/Xcode.app`.
2. `npm run ios:open` builds the phone app and opens the project in Xcode.
3. In Xcode, select the **App** target → **Signing & Capabilities** → **Team** → add your Apple ID (a free one works). If Xcode says the bundle identifier is taken, change `com.studywise.courses` to something of your own, like `com.yourname.studywise`.
4. Plug in the iPhone (or have it on the same Wi-Fi), turn on Settings → Privacy & Security → **Developer Mode** on the phone, pick it as the run destination at the top of Xcode, and press ▶︎.
5. The first time, the phone asks you to trust the developer: Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → your Apple ID → Trust.
6. Pair it: Settings → Devices on the computer → Pair a device, then Settings → Sync on the iPhone with the address and code; allow local network access when iOS asks.

With a free Apple ID the app stops opening after 7 days; run it from Xcode again to renew it (your data stays on the phone and on the computer). A paid Apple Developer account ($99 a year) makes it last a year and allows TestFlight.

**Linking two computers.** Settings → Devices → **Link to another computer** moves your library from one computer to another, and keeps the two in sync after that, with no file to export or carry and no marketplace account. On the computer that has the library, turn on syncing and press **Pair a device**; on the other, type its address and code. This computer then pairs exactly as a phone does and syncs the same thirteen tables both ways (`src/main/sync/link.ts`), so nothing is replaced: the two libraries are merged. It also copies the files the rows need, which a phone doesn't keep: each course's files (slides, PDFs, transcripts, which figures are rendered from and topics are written from) and pasted images, missing ones fetched from the other computer and ones it lacks sent to it (`POST /have`, `GET`/`PUT /course-file`, `/file`; up to 45 MB a file). Course file rows store the full path on the computer that added the file, so rows from the other computer are pointed at `courses/<course>/files/<name>` here without logging it as a change, and each computer keeps its own paths (`localizeCoursePaths`, `src/main/sync/files.ts`). It syncs when the app starts, a few seconds after a change, every minute, and every two minutes while the other computer can't be reached. Changes from phones paired with the linked computer are passed on too (`syncWith` with `relay`). A computer can't link to one that's already linked to it. Unlinking stops syncing; both keep everything. Both computers need the same version of Studywise.

**Pairing.** Settings → Devices on the computer turns on a small HTTP server on the local network (port 47821) and shows the computer's addresses. Pair a device shows a 6-digit code that works once, for 10 minutes, and is cancelled after 5 wrong tries. The phone gets a token (the computer keeps only its hash); everything except saying hello and pairing needs it. Unpairing on either side stops syncing; the phone keeps its copy. The server never opens dialogs for the phone, and it won't run anything that touches files on the computer, backups, settings or the Ollama key.

## Code layout

`src/main` is the Electron main process: database, Claude bridge, jobs, import/export and IPC. `src/preload` exposes `window.studywise.<area>.<method>()` to the page. `src/renderer` is the React UI. `src/shared` has the types, the StudySmarter parser, the schedulers, marking and the keymap. The page runs sandboxed with context isolation, a strict CSP and no remote content, and every IPC message is checked with zod.

Other scripts: `npm run ai:real-check` (one real call per task, plus notes and cards from a 12-page PDF), `node scripts/packaged-check.mjs` (a real generation through a built app with a Finder-like PATH), `npm run icons`, and `node scripts/screenshots.mjs [--dark]`.

## Time studied and the home page

While a topic, flashcards, Learn or a note is open, the window is in front and you've touched the keyboard, mouse or screen in the last three minutes, the time is counted and saved about once a minute (one row per day per device, so it syncs between the phone and the computer without clashing). The home page shows today's time with the last seven days as bars, then total time, the last 30 days, your study streak (days with studying or reviews), topics done, topic questions answered and how many were right first time, your topic test average, cards reviewed, and retention (reviews in the last 30 days not rated Again).
